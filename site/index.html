    How the picks are made

    Each projection starts with the player's recent scoring, then adjusts for this week's opponent and game outlook. It's a simple, transparent model, not a crystal ball.

    • Form: this season's points per game, with recent games weighted more. Early in the season it's blended with last season's average so a few games don't swing it too far.
    • Matchup: how many points this week's opponent has allowed to the position compared with the league average. Defenses with only a few games are pulled toward average. Capped at ±20%.
    • Team total: the team's Vegas implied points for the game (from the over/under and spread). Higher-scoring games mean more fantasy points. Capped at ±15%.
    • Usage: whether the player's targets and carries (attempts and carries for QBs) over the last three games are up or down versus his season average. Capped at ±10%.
    • Injuries: players listed Out, Doubtful, or on IR are left off. Questionable players stay on with a warning. If a teammate ahead of a player on the depth chart is out, his projection gets a 10% bump.

    Not included: weather, coaching changes, or news after the last update. Players on bye are left out, and players who haven't played in three weeks are dropped. Check the latest news before you set a lineup.

    Break down by

    Roles are set each week by how much each player has been used by his team so far this season: targets for WRs and TEs, carries plus targets for RBs. So WR1 is the offense's top receiver, even in a quiet week. If a team had no WR5 that week, the defense allowed 0 to that role.

      How waiver picks are made

      First, the page works out who is probably already rostered. In a 12-team league with 16-man rosters, teams usually hold about 19 QBs, 55 RBs, 62 WRs, and 19 TEs in total; smaller leagues hold proportionally fewer, so more good players are free. The most-rostered players at each position are treated as taken, using ESPN's % rostered when it's available and an estimate from last season's and this season's scoring when it isn't.

      Everyone left is ranked by expected points per week: 45% next week's projection, 35% his last three games, and 20% his longer-term form. In the All view, players are ranked by how much they beat a typical starter at their position in your league size, so a QB isn't ranked higher just because QBs score more. Players get a 10% boost when a teammate ahead of them on the depth chart is out. Players who are out, doubtful, on IR, or haven't played in three weeks are skipped.
